Guideline Changes Atlas Hematology · Vol. 5 — Andexanet Withdrawal
What Changed · December 22, 2025

Andexanet alfa is off the US market — 4F-PCC is now the default

Seven years after becoming the first FDA-approved factor Xa inhibitor reversal agent, andexanet alfa (Andexxa) was voluntarily withdrawn from US commercial sale after postmarketing data showed its thrombotic risk outweighed its benefit. Four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate (4F-PCC, Kcentra) — long the off-label fallback — is now the de facto standard of care for apixaban and rivaroxaban reversal in the US.

02 · Timeline

How we got here

May 2018
FDA grants accelerated approval to andexanet alfa based on anti-factor Xa activity change in healthy volunteers — a surrogate endpoint. Approval includes a boxed warning for thromboembolic risk and a requirement to conduct a confirmatory RCT.
2024
ANNEXA-I published (N Engl J Med 2024;390:1745–1755): 530 patients with factor Xa inhibitor-associated intracerebral hemorrhage randomized to andexanet vs. usual care (predominantly 4F-PCC). Trial stopped early for efficacy after a preplanned interim analysis of 450 patients.
November 21, 2024
FDA's Cellular, Tissue, and Gene Therapies Advisory Committee meets to review postmarketing thromboembolic safety data.
December 18, 2025
FDA issues a safety communication: based on postmarketing data on serious and fatal thromboembolic events, the agency "considers the risks of the product to outweigh its benefits."
December 22, 2025
AstraZeneca ends US commercial sales and manufacturing of Andexxa. Withdrawal is voluntary and framed as "for commercial reasons" after the company and FDA could not agree on a path from accelerated to traditional approval.
03 · Deep Dive

Why it was withdrawn: the ANNEXA-I signal

ANNEXA-I trial Safety signal, trial stopped early

530 patients, factor Xa inhibitor-associated ICH, mean age 79, 87% on anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation

Patients presented within 6 hours of ICH symptom onset and within 15 hours of their last apixaban, edoxaban, or rivaroxaban dose. The trial met its efficacy endpoint early — effective hemostasis at 12 hours was better with andexanet — but the safety data told a different story:

14.6%
Any thrombotic event, andexanet arm
6.9%
Any thrombotic event, usual care arm
2.5%
Thrombosis-related death by day 30, andexanet arm (6 patients)
0.9%
Thrombosis-related death by day 30, usual care arm (2 patients)

The pattern extended to specific ischemic events, including a higher rate of myocardial infarction in the andexanet arm relative to usual care. The FDA's conclusion, stated plainly in its safety communication: the accumulated postmarketing thromboembolic risk — including fatal outcomes — outweighs the drug's clinical benefit.

04 · Deep Dive

What to use now: 4F-PCC

4F-PCC (Kcentra) De facto standard of care

Off-label for factor Xa inhibitor reversal — FDA-approved indication remains vitamin K antagonist reversal

4F-PCC was already the most common real-world comparator in ANNEXA-I's "usual care" arm and has years of retrospective use for apixaban/rivaroxaban-associated bleeding, particularly ICH and pre-urgent-surgery reversal. With andexanet gone from the US market, it is now the default reversal strategy for life-threatening bleeding or urgent surgery in patients on apixaban or rivaroxaban.

⚠ Order-set and workflow check

Do not order Andexxa — it is no longer commercially available in the US and cannot be obtained through normal supply channels. Confirm your ED and pharmacy order sets, massive transfusion protocols, and neurosurgery/ICH pathways have been updated to route directly to 4F-PCC rather than defaulting to a now-nonexistent option.

05 · Evidence Caveat

4F-PCC's mechanism has real limits — know them

4F-PCC is a nonspecific factor-replacement strategy, not a targeted antidote. In vitro thrombin-generation studies found 4F-PCC could fully normalize thrombin generation only at relatively low factor Xa inhibitor concentrations — below roughly 75 ng/mL depending on the specific parameter measured — and was unable to normalize thrombin generation at the higher inhibitor concentrations tested (75–500 ng/mL). This is a mechanistic caveat worth knowing, not a reason to withhold 4F-PCC: it's currently the best available option, and retrospective clinical data have found it performs reasonably against andexanet despite the theoretical mechanistic gap.

⚡ What the comparative outcome data actually show

Retrospective and propensity-matched comparisons of andexanet vs. 4F-PCC for factor Xa inhibitor-associated ICH have produced mixed signals across studies — some show no significant difference in hemostatic outcomes, and at least one retrospective analysis reported lower in-hospital mortality with 4F-PCC. None of this is randomized, head-to-head, prospective data — treat outcome comparisons between the two as hypothesis-generating, not definitive, per ISTH Scientific and Standardization Committee guidance on DOAC reversal.

06 · On the Horizon

What's coming — and what isn't changing

Ciraparantag Investigational

A small synthetic molecule proposed as a "universal" reversal agent — binding factor Xa inhibitors, dabigatran, and heparins alike without a procoagulant effect of its own. It remains in clinical development; no confirmed US approval timeline is established as of this writing. Don't expect it to fill the andexanet gap in the near term.

Idarucizumab (Praxbind) Unaffected

Dabigatran reversal is untouched by this withdrawal — idarucizumab is a different drug on a different approval pathway, targeting a direct thrombin inhibitor rather than a factor Xa inhibitor. Continue using it as before.

✓ Verify before use

Andexanet remains available outside the US (conditionally approved in Europe; approved in the UK and Japan under the brand name Ondexxya). If you're consulting on a patient transferred internationally or reviewing outside records, don't assume andexanet's absence from a US chart means it was never available to that patient.

07 · Bottom Line

For the bedside

  • Andexanet alfa cannot be ordered in the US as of December 22, 2025 — don't page pharmacy asking for it.
  • 4F-PCC (Kcentra) is the reversal strategy for life-threatening bleeding or urgent surgery in patients on apixaban or rivaroxaban — this is now standard practice, not an off-label workaround.
  • Idarucizumab for dabigatran is unaffected — nothing changes there.
  • Update ICH and massive-transfusion pathways so they don't still list andexanet as a default order.
  • Ciraparantag is not a near-term substitute — it remains investigational.
  • The underlying lesson generalizes: accelerated-approval drugs carry real uncertainty until the confirmatory trial reports — this is what that process looks like when the confirmatory data cuts against the drug.
