What Changed · June 2024

The first DKA/HHS consensus update in 15 years

The ADA's joint consensus report with EASD, JBDS, AACE, and DST — its first revision since 2009 — brings quantitative beta-hydroxybutyrate into the diagnostic and resolution criteria, endorses subcutaneous insulin for mild-to-moderate DKA outside the ICU, and defines HHS resolution criteria for the first time ever.

02 · Deep Dive

Beta-hydroxybutyrate joins the diagnostic core

Quantitative BHB testing New diagnostic criterion

The report's most consequential change: quantitative beta-hydroxybutyrate measurement — ideally via bedside/point-of-care testing — is now incorporated into the diagnostic criteria for both DKA and HHS, and is also used to assess severity and determine resolution. This moves DKA assessment beyond anion gap and bicarbonate alone, both of which can be affected by comorbid conditions and are slower to normalize than the underlying ketone burden in some patients.

03 · Deep Dive

Not every DKA needs an ICU insulin drip

Subcutaneous insulin for mild/uncomplicated moderate DKA New recommendation

When clinically appropriate, mild and uncomplicated moderate DKA can now be managed with subcutaneous rapid-acting insulin analogues every 1–2 hours in a noncritical care setting, with close nursing supervision — rather than defaulting every DKA patient to an ICU-level IV insulin infusion. This is a disposition-relevant change: it opens a pathway for appropriately selected patients to avoid ICU admission.

✓ Verify before use

"Mild and uncomplicated moderate" is doing real work in that sentence — severe DKA, hemodynamic instability, altered mental status, or significant comorbidity still point toward IV insulin and a higher level of care. Confirm your institution's DKA pathway has defined the severity cutoffs for the subcutaneous option before relying on it.

04 · Deep Dive

HHS finally has resolution criteria

HHS resolution criteria First-ever, 2024

Until this update, HHS had no formally defined resolution criteria — a real gap given how different its physiology is from DKA. The 2024 report also updates DKA resolution criteria: glucose <200 mg/dL, serum bicarbonate ≥18 mEq/L, and venous pH >7.3. Throughout, the report emphasizes clinical judgment over rigid numeric thresholds when making disposition and insulin-transition decisions.

05 · Deep Dive

Balanced solutions may resolve DKA faster than saline

Fluid choice Evolving evidence

Recent evidence cited alongside the consensus update suggests balanced electrolyte solutions may resolve DKA faster than 0.9% saline — a mean difference of roughly 5.36 fewer hours to resolution in the cited comparison. Standard initial fluid resuscitation (isotonic saline 15–20 mL/kg/hr, correcting an estimated deficit of ~6 L over 24 hours, with 50% of the deficit replaced in the first 8–12 hours) remains the anchor; the balanced-solution signal is a real evolution in the underlying evidence rather than a formal guideline mandate to abandon saline.

06 · Other Endocrine Updates

Elsewhere in endocrinology, 2024–2025

⚡ 2025 ATA differentiated thyroid cancer guideline

The American Thyroid Association's first update to its differentiated thyroid cancer guideline since 2015 introduces a "DATA" framework (Diagnosis, risk/benefit Assessment, Treatment decisions, response Assessment), expands active surveillance and lobectomy over total thyroidectomy for select low-risk disease, and de-escalates surveillance intensity for low-risk patients. This is oncology/endocrinology follow-up territory rather than an acute-care change — flagged here for completeness, not deep-dived, since it doesn't change ED management of a thyroid nodule found incidentally.

07 · Bottom Line

For the bedside

  • If your lab offers bedside beta-hydroxybutyrate, it now belongs in the DKA/HHS workup alongside anion gap and bicarbonate — not just as a research curiosity.
  • A well-appearing patient with mild or uncomplicated moderate DKA may be a candidate for subcutaneous insulin and a floor bed rather than an automatic ICU admission — but confirm your institution's pathway defines that boundary before triaging on it.
  • HHS now has actual resolution criteria to chart toward, rather than an open-ended "until it looks better."
  • Mixed DKA/HHS features get treated as DKA, with the higher 0.1 units/kg/hr starting insulin rate.
  • Balanced electrolyte solutions are a reasonable fluid choice in DKA resuscitation — the evidence is trending that direction, even without a hard guideline mandate yet.
