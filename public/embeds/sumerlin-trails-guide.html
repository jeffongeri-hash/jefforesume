The revised priorities

Updated from homeowner feedback. Existing providers stay in place; new bids focus on the work that remains.

Bathrooms & repairs

Assess musty tubs and failed finishes. Bid each affected bathroom as a complete room. Handle garage drywall and torn screens in parallel.

Flooring & painting

Investigate failed tile before replacing it. Coordinate demolition, underlying repairs, painting, flooring and final touch-ups to avoid rework.

Kitchen later

Price kitchen upgrades separately after bathroom needs are understood. Keep pool construction and decorative extras as future options.

Already covered: Peterman Brothers handles the existing plumbing/HVAC membership and annual checks. Keep the current landscaper for weekly mowing during the growing season. Arrange driveway shoveling with a neighborhood helper; neither mowing nor snow service has been booked through this guide.

Make the bids comparable

One complete scope per bathroom

  1. Record the tub/shower condition, odor assessment, ventilation and surrounding damage.
  2. List what stays and what comes out: demolition, tub/shower, waterproofing, tile, subfloor preparation, vanity, fixtures, drywall, trim and paint.
  3. Set product allowances, disposal costs and unit prices for concealed repairs.
  4. Name the lead contractor, trade and permit responsibilities, payment milestones, change-order process and warranty terms.
  5. Keep at least one bathroom usable during the work.

Prevent repeat work

Failed tile: investigate why the existing assembly failed and specify the preparation method before choosing replacement tile.

Garage: measure damaged drywall; include joint finishing, primer and paint. Preserve any required garage separation assembly.

Mesh/screens: confirm whether these are window, door or porch screens, then compare re-meshing against frame replacement.

Fence: ask BlueBird to repair sound sections first. Identify the exact wood treatment before requesting HOA guidance.

Kitchen: plan future floor transitions now, but keep construction in a later phase.

Garden planning

Start with the growing location, sunlight, access to water and any standing-water problem. Submit a scaled backyard-bed plan before installation. Outdoor beds or patio containers avoid relying on a new shed.

Earlier planning allowances: containers $100–$350; two 4 × 8-foot DIY beds $500–$1,400; professionally installed beds $1,000–$2,500. These are analyst allowances, not contractor quotes; major drainage/grading is excluded.

An indoor grow-light shelf was estimated at $150–$500 plus electricity. Crop choice, light, ventilation and available space determine feasibility.
