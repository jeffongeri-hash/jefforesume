The decisions that matter first

  • New sheds, outbuildings, and above‑ground pools are prohibited outright by the published covenants — don't let a contractor tell you otherwise.
  • Backyard gardens and fences need HOA approval, with real location and design limits attached.
  • Household pets are allowed if confined and not a nuisance; an in‑ground pool has no express blanket approval and should be treated as a full proposal requiring written review.

Fence rules (secs. 6.16 & 6.27)

Fence issueAllowed / not allowed / conditions
Approval & timingWritten ARC approval before installation. Submit location, material, color, height and a drawing/photo at least 30 days before work. No written approval within 30 days = denied.
LocationNo ordinary front-yard fence. No fence closer to the front lot line than the residence's rear foundation line. No fence between the front setback and street curb; none in the public right-of-way.
Listed materialsVinyl-coated chain link, wrought iron, cedar or treated pine. Solid vinyl/PVC panels and aluminum are not listed — don't assume they qualify.
Wood style / finishText calls for dog-eared, flat-top shadow-box style, 1×6-inch vertical boards, remaining unpainted. Confirm how the ARC applies this to listed alternatives — stain is not expressly resolved.
Special 5-foot limitRear-yard fences on lots adjacent to E. 116th St, E. 121st St, or Hoosier Rd must be wood shadow-box style, no more than 5 feet high. No universal height limit was found for other lots.
Visibility / upkeep / DIYDon't block traffic sight lines 2–9 ft above street level within the 25-ft corner triangle, or within 10 ft of a street/driveway/alley intersection (sec. 6.16). Maintain the fence; approved DIY work must meet professional standards and may require inspection.
Drainage-easement amendmentAmended sec. 3.8 allows approved fences that don't impede drainage — not a blanket easement approval. Preserve access and check the plat/city requirements. PDF pp.30–31.

Prepare one package for the HOA and contractors

  • Exact address, survey/plot plan, photos, and measured dimensions — mark the rear foundation line, property boundaries, drainage paths and easements.
  • Fence: material, height, board/profile, finish, gates, and a drawing/photo. Ask the HOA to resolve any material/style ambiguity in writing.
  • Garden beds: number, size, depth, materials, location, irrigation, and any proposed screening. A small patio container plan may fit the stated exception; a backyard bed plan needs review.
  • Pool: request the pool, excavation, disposal, electrical/bonding, drainage, decking, and fencing/barriers as separate bid components.
  • Interior work: five bedrooms and three baths alone are not enough for a firm quote — identify the actual rooms, measurements, fixtures, finish level, and any layout/plumbing changes.
