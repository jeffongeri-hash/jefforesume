What Changed · 2024–2026

Three ACC/AHA guidelines rewrote ACS, blood pressure, and lipid management

In a 13-month span, the ACC/AHA replaced three cornerstone cardiology guidelines: a unified Acute Coronary Syndromes guideline (Feb 2025) co-authored with ACEP and NAEMSP, a Hypertension guideline (Aug 2025) that swaps the risk calculator EDs have used for a decade, and a Dyslipidemia guideline (March 2026) that brings explicit LDL-C numeric targets back after the 2018 guideline moved away from them.

01 · Overview

At a glance

Three guidelines, one underlying theme: risk stratification got more granular everywhere. ACS antithrombotic therapy is now bleeding-risk-adjusted, hypertension treatment thresholds now run through a better-calibrated CVD risk equation, and lipid targets are explicit numbers again instead of risk-category ranges.

03 · Deep Dive

ACS: one guideline replaces four

2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI Guideline New unified structure

Retires the 2013 STEMI, 2014 NSTEMI, 2015 primary-PCI focused update, and 2016 DAPT-duration guidelines — with EM and EMS physician societies as co-authors for the first time

This is the guideline most directly written with emergency physicians and EMS at the table. Reaffirmed cornerstones: ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact for suspected ACS, high-sensitivity troponin to accelerate both rule-in and rule-out, and primary PCI within 90 minutes (120 for transfer) as the preferred reperfusion strategy when achievable.

For risk stratification: intermediate/high-risk NSTE-ACS gets an invasive strategy with intent to revascularize during the index hospitalization; low-risk NSTE-ACS can use either a routine or selective invasive approach with further risk stratification. Radial access is preferred over femoral to reduce bleeding, vascular complications, and death. High-intensity statin is recommended for all ACS patients, with ezetimibe as an add-on or as an alternative for statin-intolerant patients.

⚠ Fondaparinux + PCI

The guideline gives a Class 3 (harm) recommendation against fondaparinux during PCI specifically, due to guide catheter thrombosis risk — a sharper line than prior guidance. Fondaparinux otherwise remains reasonable in non-invasive management pathways; the contraindication is specific to the catheterization lab.

04 · Deep Dive

Hypertension: the risk calculator changed, and so did who gets treated

2025 ACC/AHA High Blood Pressure Guideline New risk tool

Replaces the 2017 guideline; diagnostic categories and the 130/80 threshold are unchanged

The Pooled Cohort Equations — which overestimated risk roughly 2-fold relative to observed events — are replaced by the PREVENT equations: better calibrated, estimate both 10- and 30-year risk, incorporate kidney and metabolic measures alongside cardiovascular risk, extend to a broader age range, and drop race as an input variable.

Stage 2 (≥140/90)
Pharmacologic therapy for all — unchanged
Stage 1 (130–139/80–89)
Treat if PREVENT 10-yr ≥7.5%, or clinical CVD/diabetes/CKD; otherwise 3–6 month lifestyle trial first
⚡ Equity-related language change

The prior guideline's language preferring calcium channel blockers or thiazide diuretics specifically for Black patients without heart failure or CKD has been removed. First-line options — thiazide-type diuretics, CCBs, ACE inhibitors, or ARBs — are now presented without race-based preference language.

Two other practical changes: urine albumin-creatinine ratio (UACR) testing moves from optional to recommended for all patients with high BP, and renal denervation becomes a formal consideration for resistant hypertension. CKD management is reaffirmed — ACEi/ARB preferred, goal SBP <130.

⚠ Directly relevant to the ED: don't chase inpatient asymptomatic severe hypertension

Hospitalized patients with asymptomatic severe hypertension often have spontaneous BP reduction. Aggressive IV or as-needed antihypertensive treatment in this setting is associated with increased mortality, acute kidney injury, and longer hospital stays — the guideline explicitly cautions against treating the number in a patient without end-organ symptoms.

05 · Deep Dive

Dyslipidemia: numeric LDL-C goals are back

2026 ACC/AHA/Multisociety Dyslipidemia Guideline Restored numeric targets

Retitled from "Blood Cholesterol" to "Dyslipidemia" — replaces the 2018 guideline, evidence reviewed through late 2024

The 2018 guideline moved toward risk-category-based statin intensity rather than explicit numeric LDL-C targets. The 2026 guideline reverses that: explicit goals return, now indexed to PREVENT-ASCVD risk.

Risk tierLDL-C goalNon-HDL-C goal
Clinical ASCVD / very high risk (secondary prevention)<55 mg/dL<85 mg/dL
High risk, PREVENT 10-yr ASCVD ≥10%<70 mg/dL<100 mg/dL
⚡ Flagging a reporting inconsistency

Some society commentary describes a broader "<100 across the board, <70 with risk factors or prior events" framing rather than the two-tier structure above. The core direction — earlier treatment, lower numeric targets, PREVENT-based risk stratification — is consistent across sources; the exact tier boundaries are worth confirming against the full published guideline before setting institutional order-set thresholds.

Biomarker use expands: apolipoprotein B (apoB) and lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] get larger roles in risk refinement, five newly FDA-approved lipid-lowering therapies are incorporated, and the guideline endorses universal lifelong lipid screening starting earlier in life rather than risk-triggered testing alone. Statin therapy remains an essential adjunct to lifestyle changes for persistently elevated triglycerides; TG ≥1000 mg/dL specifically triggers pancreatitis-prevention-focused therapy regardless of ASCVD risk status.

06 · Safety Flags Summary

Three flags worth institutional attention

⚠ Fondaparinux during PCI

Class 3 (harm) — guide catheter thrombosis risk. Confirm cath lab order sets reflect this.

⚠ Aggressive treatment of inpatient asymptomatic severe hypertension

Associated with increased mortality, AKI, and longer stays. Treat the patient, not the number, absent end-organ symptoms.

⚠ Severe hypertriglyceridemia (≥1000 mg/dL)

Pancreatitis risk threshold — triggers TG-lowering therapy independent of ASCVD risk calculations.

07 · Methodology

How each guideline was built

All three followed the ACC/AHA Joint Committee on Clinical Practice Guidelines process, with multisociety co-authorship and class-of-recommendation/level-of-evidence grading. The ACS guideline drew on a literature search from July 2023–April 2024 and is the first in this trio to include ACEP and NAEMSP as full co-authoring societies rather than solely reviewers, reflecting its emphasis on prehospital and ED-phase care.

08 · Bottom Line

For the bedside

  • ECG within 10 minutes, high-sensitivity troponin, PPCI within 90/120 minutes — the ED-phase fundamentals didn't change, they got reaffirmed with EM societies co-signing.
  • Ask about bleeding risk before assuming 12-month DAPT is the plan — high-bleeding-risk patients may be headed toward 1-month ticagrelor monotherapy instead.
  • Don't reach for fondaparinux if the patient is heading to the cath lab.
  • When you calculate cardiovascular risk now, it should be PREVENT, not the Pooled Cohort Equations — and the inputs include kidney and metabolic measures.
  • Resist the urge to aggressively treat asymptomatic severe hypertension in admitted patients without end-organ symptoms.
  • If you're counseling on lipid goals, numeric LDL-C targets are back — <55 or <70 depending on risk tier — verify against your institution's adopted thresholds.
