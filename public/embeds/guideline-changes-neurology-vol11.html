What Changed · 2026

Tenecteplase is now equal-footing with alteplase for acute stroke

The first full AHA/ASA acute ischemic stroke guideline rewrite since 2018/2019 gives tenecteplase a Class 1 recommendation alongside alteplase, extends thrombolysis eligibility out to 24 hours for select large-vessel-occlusion patients who can't get thrombectomy, and pushes systems toward direct transport to thrombectomy-capable centers.

02 · Deep Dive

Why tenecteplase moved to Class 1

Tenecteplase vs. alteplase, standard 4.5-hour window Upgraded to Class 1

Multiple international noninferiority trials accumulated since the 2019 update, and the AcT trial (Canada, 1,577 patients) found tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg) noninferior to standard-dose alteplase for AIS within 4.5 hours, with similar safety outcomes. The 2026 guideline reflects this evidence base with a straightforward Class 1 recommendation: use either tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg or alteplase 0.9 mg/kg to improve functional outcomes in patients presenting within 4.5 hours — no longer restricted to minor-deficit or thrombectomy-eligible subgroups.

⚡ Why many centers are already moving to TNK

Tenecteplase is given as a single IV bolus rather than a bolus-plus-one-hour-infusion — a real practical advantage for door-to-needle time, interfacility transfer, and reducing infusion-pump-related delays. The guideline authors explicitly note this ease-of-use as a factor in the accelerating shift toward TNK, independent of the noninferiority data itself.

The guideline also emphasizes administering thrombolysis as quickly as possible in eligible patients with disabling deficits presenting within 4.5 hours, regardless of NIHSS score and without requiring advanced imaging — reinforcing speed over additional imaging workup in the standard window.

03 · Deep Dive

Thrombolysis windows extended for select patients

Extended-window IV thrombolysis New recommendations

Wake-up stroke or unknown onset, salvageable tissue on advanced imagingWithin 9h of sleep midpoint, or 4.5–9h from last known well2a — reasonable
LVO with salvageable tissue, unable to undergo EVT4.5–24h from last known well2b — may be beneficial

These build on the EXTEND and TRACE-3 trials: in TRACE-3, alteplase given 4.5–9 hours after onset (or after waking with symptoms) increased the proportion of patients with minor or no neurologic deficit compared to placebo. In EXTEND, tenecteplase given 4.5–24 hours after onset in LVO patients who didn't undergo EVT resulted in less disability and similar survival compared to antiplatelet therapy alone — though with a higher rate of intracranial hemorrhage.

04 · Evidence Caveat

Not every late-window trial was positive — know which agent, which trial

⚠ TIMELESS was negative

The TIMELESS trial specifically tested tenecteplase in the late window and did not show benefit. The positive late-window signals (HOPE, TRACE-3) come from trials using alteplase, in populations where few patients underwent mechanical thrombectomy. Don't extrapolate late-window eligibility criteria across agents interchangeably — the evidence base differs by drug and by trial population, and the guideline's extended-window recommendations reflect that nuance rather than a blanket "any thrombolytic, any time up to 24 hours" rule.

05 · Deep Dive

Direct transport to EVT-capable centers, system-dependent

The guideline endorses considering direct transport to the closest endovascular-thrombectomy-capable hospital, based on local system-of-care characteristics — in the absence of well-functioning systems with rapid interhospital transfer processes. This is a system-design recommendation more than an individual clinical order: whether "drip and ship" to the nearest stroke-ready hospital or direct transport to a comprehensive/thrombectomy center is better depends on your region's actual transfer speed, not a universal rule.

Additional 2026 updates outside the thrombolysis discussion: refined approaches to hyperglycemia and dysphagia management in the acute phase, a dedicated pediatric AIS section, and a modified approach to thrombolysis contraindications — reflecting a decade of accumulated safety data narrowing what actually needs to exclude a patient from treatment.

06 · Bottom Line

For the bedside

  • Tenecteplase and alteplase are both Class 1, first-line options within 4.5 hours — know which one your institution has adopted and why.
  • Don't withhold thrombolysis in the standard window while waiting on advanced imaging or agonizing over a low NIHSS in a patient with a genuinely disabling deficit.
  • Extended-window thrombolysis (up to 24h) is real but narrow — salvageable tissue on advanced imaging, specific patient populations, and agent-specific evidence all matter. This is not a blanket late-window green light.
  • Remember TIMELESS was negative for late-window tenecteplase specifically — the late-window data supporting benefit is largely alteplase-based.
  • Direct-to-EVT-center transport decisions are a system-of-care question, not a fixed universal protocol — know your region's actual transfer time.
