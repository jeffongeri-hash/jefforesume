Section 3.1

Implied Volatility Should Be Above Realized Volatility

Bennett argues that implied volatility can rationally exceed expected realized volatility because short-volatility positions are exposed to equity risk. Hedging demand can add an additional premium.

← PreviousChapter 3Next →

Illustrated intuition

The implied-realized gap is not automatically free money. Short vol tends to lose when equities fall, so part of the premium compensates for bearing bad-state risk.

Reading lens: separate the payoff shape from the volatility/correlation assumptions that determine its price.

Key takeaways

  • Short vol behaves partly like long equity risk.
  • Risk-neutral pricing can place implied above expected realized.
  • Hedging demand can push implied volatility higher still.
