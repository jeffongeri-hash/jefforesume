Section 5.5

Contingent Premium Options

The buyer pays no premium initially, but a larger premium becomes due if a trigger condition is met. Bennett notes their use for zero-upfront-cost crash protection.

← PreviousChapter 5Next →

Illustrated intuition

The price is not free—it is deferred and conditional. If the feared market path happens, the premium bill arrives exactly when protection becomes relevant.

Reading lens: separate the payoff shape from the volatility/correlation assumptions that determine its price.

Key takeaways

  • Zero upfront premium does not mean zero economic cost.
  • Trigger probability determines the larger conditional premium.
  • Popular form: OTM downside protection.
