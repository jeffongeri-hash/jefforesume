What Changed · 2024–2025

A device joined the PPH algorithm and a blood test entered the preeclampsia workup

ACOG issued two Clinical Practice Updates that change what's available at the bedside for the two obstetric emergencies EM sees most: postpartum hemorrhage now has a formally recognized nonsurgical vacuum-induced device option, and hospitalized patients with hypertensive disorders of pregnancy now have a biomarker test to help predict progression to severe preeclampsia.

02 · Deep Dive

A vacuum-induced device enters the PPH algorithm

Nonsurgical hemorrhage-control devices Formally incorporated, Oct 2025

Focused update to Practice Bulletin No. 183, Postpartum Hemorrhage (2017)

The Jada System is an FDA-cleared intrauterine vacuum-induced hemorrhage-control device: a soft silicone intrauterine loop lined with vacuum pores, a cervical seal, and tubing connected to an external vacuum source. It's placed transvaginally and manually after vaginal delivery, or following hysterotomy closure at cesarean, with the internal loop seated at the fundus and the cervical seal inflated with 60–120 mL sterile water.

800 patients
RUBY study, 16 US hospitals, Oct 2020–March 2022
92.5%
Treatment success, vaginal birth
83.7%
Treatment success, cesarean birth
⚠ Know the contraindications

Not for retained products of conception or placenta accreta spectrum. Requires at least 3 cm cervical dilation — cannot be placed via an open hysterotomy. Abnormal uterine anatomy and intrauterine infection are contraindications. Data in cesarean deliveries and in uterine cavities smaller than a 34-week gestation are more limited than for vaginal delivery.

03 · Deep Dive

A biomarker enters the preeclampsia workup

sFlt-1/PlGF ratio New complementary tool, June 2024

Updates Practice Bulletin No. 222, Gestational Hypertension and Preeclampsia (2020)

Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1 (sFlt-1, antiangiogenic) and placental growth factor (PlGF, proangiogenic) are placental proteins whose ratio shifts with abnormal placentation. In 2023, this became the first sFlt-1/PlGF assay to receive FDA marketing approval in the US. ACOG's June 2024 update positions the ratio as a complementary risk-stratification tool — not a replacement for clinical criteria — specifically for patients hospitalized between 23 and 35 weeks' gestation with hypertensive disorders, to help predict short-term progression to preeclampsia with severe features.

~79%
Positive predictive value, real-world cohort (n=65, single center)
~94%
Negative predictive value, same cohort
⚡ Scope, not a screening tool

This is for risk-stratifying patients already hospitalized for a hypertensive disorder in a specific gestational window — it is not validated as a general ED triage screen for every pregnant patient with elevated blood pressure. Core diagnostic criteria for gestational hypertension and preeclampsia (PB 222) are unchanged; the biomarker adds to, rather than replaces, that framework.

05 · Bottom Line

For the bedside

  • Know that a vacuum-induced intrauterine device is now a formally recognized nonsurgical PPH option — useful context if you're stabilizing before transfer or consulting OB by phone.
  • The device isn't for retained POC, accreta spectrum, or cervices under 3 cm dilated — don't suggest it in those scenarios.
  • If a hospitalized HDP patient at 23–35 weeks has an sFlt-1/PlGF result available, treat it as one more data point layered onto clinical criteria, not a stand-alone rule-in or rule-out.
  • Core preeclampsia diagnostic criteria (BP thresholds, proteinuria, severe features) haven't changed — don't let biomarker availability shift your bedside diagnostic threshold.
