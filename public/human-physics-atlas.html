01 · HEMODYNAMICS

Pressure, radius and resistance

For the idealized laminar, rigid tube, flow follows Q ∝ ΔP × r⁴ / (L × μ). Actual circulation is pulsatile, branching, elastic and non-Newtonian, but the fourth-power radius term explains why arteriolar tone has such large hemodynamic consequences.

03 · BIOMECHANICS

Force, moment arm and torque

External torque = force × perpendicular moment arm. Because muscles often operate with short internal moment arms, tendon and joint-contact forces may substantially exceed the external load. A hinge joint predominantly flexes/extends but the knee also experiences rotation and translation under load.

04 · GRAVITY AND FALLS

Center of mass must stay recoverable

Static stability improves when the center-of-mass projection remains within the base of support. During gait it repeatedly moves beyond the stance footprint; recovery requires a rapid step, traction, vision, vestibular/proprioceptive input and adequate muscle response.

Teaching cue: a wider stance and a closer load reduce instability and moment arm. Sedating drugs, poor lighting, uneven surfaces, delayed reaction and impaired sensory inputs raise fall risk. Not individualized rehabilitation advice.

05 · KINETIC MOVEMENT AND INJURY MECHANISM

Impulse and deceleration

Impulse changes momentum. If a given momentum change occurs over a longer time, average force falls. Controlled deceleration and compliant surfaces can therefore reduce peak loading; mechanism alone does not establish injury severity.

Twist plus load

A planted foot with tibial rotation plus valgus or anterior shear can load knee stabilizers. Force direction, speed, flexion angle, tissue state and individual anatomy determine the resulting injury pattern.

Posture and venous return

Standing creates a dependent hydrostatic column. Venous valves and skeletal-muscle contraction support venous return while autonomic reflexes buffer blood-pressure change. Orthostatic symptoms or syncope need clinical evaluation.
