SYSTEMS COMPANION · HUMAN PHYSICS ATLAS

GI, GU, Cardiac & MSK Physics

Four linked applications of pressure gradients, compliance, resistance, Starling forces, torque and energy transfer. These are mechanism models—not diagnostic rules, IV/medication protocols, or individualized injury estimates.

GI · LUMINAL FLOW AND OUTPUT

Peristalsis moves a deformable fluid-solid mixture

Chyme is not water in a rigid pipe. Circular-muscle contraction behind a bolus and receptive relaxation ahead create a moving pressure field; segmentation mainly mixes and changes wall shear exposure. Net intestinal fluid movement is driven chiefly by epithelial solute transport, especially sodium, chloride and bicarbonate—not pressure alone.

→
  • Low resistance: a patent, compliant lumen allows propulsion and mixing.
  • Obstruction: rising proximal volume and pressure increase wall tension; secretion and swallowed air can further distend the system.
  • Output: transit time controls the opportunity for water/electrolyte absorption; rapid transit can leave more water in stool.
GU · FILTRATION, FLOW AND PRESSURE

Glomerular filtration is a Starling-force problem

Filtration depends on hydraulic pressure favoring movement into Bowman space, opposed by Bowman-space hydrostatic pressure and plasma oncotic pressure, multiplied by filtration coefficient. Tubules then reclaim or secrete solute and water; urine flow is an outcome of filtration plus tubular handling.

Illustrative net filtration-pressure index
10.0
NFP ≈ PGC − PBS − πGC; simplified, excludes Bowman-space oncotic pressure and does not calculate GFR.

A rise in downstream urinary pressure can oppose filtration; lower effective circulating volume and afferent/efferent arteriolar tone alter renal perfusion. This is not a test for pre/intra/postrenal disease.

CARDIAC CYCLE · STENOSIS

Valve narrowing creates a time-specific pressure gradient

Flow across a valve requires a pressure difference. In mitral stenosis, left-atrial pressure must exceed left-ventricular diastolic pressure long enough to fill the ventricle; reduced valve area raises the diastolic transmitral gradient, which can transmit backward toward pulmonary veins. In aortic stenosis, LV pressure must exceed aortic pressure during systole to eject through the narrowed valve.

Gradient is flow-dependent: it must be interpreted with valve area, rhythm, stroke volume and the full hemodynamic context. It is not interchangeable with disease severity in every patient.

MSK · MOMENT, ROTATION AND ENERGY

Loads are vectors, not just weight

External torque rises with force and perpendicular distance. A load held farther from the trunk increases lumbar external moment. Asymmetric lift, acceleration, rotation and a planted foot introduce multiple planes of loading; internal muscle forces needed to counter torque can make joint reaction forces much larger than the external object weight.

Illustrative external sagittal moment
58.9 N·m
Object-only static moment = mass × g × distance. Trunk weight, acceleration, posture, muscle co-contraction and 3-D asymmetry are not included.

Practical mechanism: bring a load closer, avoid sudden twisting under load, and use a stable base. These reduce external moment but do not guarantee safety or rule out injury.
